Stage 1 of 5

Read the chart — momentum & volatility

Every symbol starts with the classic technicals — the same ones most people use, but for us they're inputs, not the answer.

  • RSI — is it overbought or oversold? Momentum exhaustion.
  • MACD — is momentum shifting (crossovers, histogram)?
  • Bollinger Bands — where is price inside its volatility envelope?
  • Moving averages (50 / 150 / 200) & ADX — the trend backbone and how strong it is.
Most retail analysis ends here — one of these signals fires and they trade. We've only just started.
Stage 2 of 5

Find where price will actually react

We collect every candidate floor & ceiling — moving averages, Bollinger bands, recent swing highs/lows — and the layer almost no one has:

  • Gamma walls — strikes where options dealers are forced to hedge. The put wall acts as a magnet/floor, the call wall as a ceiling.

Nearby levels are clustered into zones (within 0.6×ATR) so a floor backed by an SMA and a put wall counts as one strong zone.

The edge most people don't have

Gamma walls come from live options open interest — actual dealer positioning, not just past price. They tell you where big money is obligated to defend a level.

Stage 3 of 5

Test the floor & ceiling — does it hold?

For each zone we count how many times price touched it and how often it held vs broke. A floor that holds 9 of 10 times is real; one tested twice is noise.

■ held   ■ broke

  • Touch count & hold-rate — e.g. TLT $87 zone: 19 touches · 15 holds → 74%.
  • Wilson-smoothed — a small sample (3/3) can't masquerade as 100%.
  • Untested zones (< 3 touches) are dropped — no track record, no trust.

Stage 5 of 5 · our USP

Is the trend friendly? — the SEPA verdict

Every options scanner can find a support level. Almost none ask the next question: is the stock actually trending up into it, or falling toward it? That's the difference between a good level and a good trade — and it's the layer we add that others don't.

What is SEPA?

Specific Entry Point Analysis — the trend template made famous by Mark Minervini, a US Investing Champion. A deterministic checklist that defines a confirmed trend — no opinion, no AI.

Six checks — price & relative strength:

  • Moving averages stacked 50 > 150 > 200, price above them
  • The 200-day is rising (slow, reliable confirmation)
  • Relative strength vs SPY is positive & rising — leading the market
  • Within 25% of the 52-week high · clearly off the low
