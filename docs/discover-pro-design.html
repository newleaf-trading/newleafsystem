1. The Problem

The existing Discover page (/workbench/discover.html) has a solid multi-stage workflow: snapshot, strategy selection, drill-in, verification, verdict. But the analysis it provides is shallow:

  • A 40-word rationale that rubber-stamps the engine's decision
  • No thesis — "why will this trade make money?" goes unanswered
  • No risk scenarios — "what could go wrong?" is never asked
  • No exit plan — the user has no idea when to close
  • No position sizing — "how many contracts?" is left to guesswork
  • No macro context — trades are analyzed in isolation from SPY/VIX/sector

A hedge fund analyst produces all of these before risking capital. Our users get a score and a sentence. The gap between "strategy builder" and "investment research" was the opportunity.

2. Our Approach

Keep the engine, add reasoning on top

The deterministic strategy engine (pipeline/strategy-engine.js) is the single source of truth. It selects strategies through a gate hierarchy (iron condor → BWB → directional → calendar → fallback). This works well — the engine discriminates correctly between 6 strategy types based on gamma, IV, and trend.

What the engine can't do is explain why, assess risk, or plan the trade lifecycle. Those require reasoning. So we added a reasoning layer on top:

Why not replace the engine with a reasoning model?
Because the engine is deterministic, auditable, and fast (30ms). A reasoning model would hallucinate strategy selections, be non-reproducible, and cost 100x more per call. The engine decides; the LLM reasons about that decision.
Why a new page instead of enhancing Discover?
Discover is a 3,070-line multi-stage wizard used by all users. Adding 6 reasoning panels would make it unwieldy and slow for users who just want a quick strategy scan. Discover Pro is a separate, deeper experience — single-page, scroll-based, aimed at users who want hedge fund-level analysis before placing a trade.

3. Architecture

System Layers

Layer 1: Fast ExplanationMarket read, strategy rationale, strike selectionqwen-plus (1.1s)1-2s

Key architectural principle

Numbers are COMPUTED. Narratives are REASONED. Never ask an LLM to do math. The P&L grid, max profit, max loss, breakevens, and position sizing are all calculated deterministically by legMath.js. The LLM provides qualitative analysis only: thesis conviction, risk scenario descriptions, exit reasoning, regime assessment.

4. Model Selection

We benchmarked 5 Qwen models on DashScope with a fixed prompt ("produce one sentence market read"). Results measure wall-clock latency, not tokens/sec. The large latency gap for 3.5/3.7 models is partly due to higher output verbosity (30x more tokens), not just slower inference.

ModelWall-clockTokens Out~tok/sUse Case
qwen-plus1.1s31~28Fast tasks market read, explanation, narrative
qwen-max1.7s34~20Previous default, replaced by qwen-plus
qwen3-max2.4s28~12Reasoning tasks thesis, risk, exit, regime, sizing
qwen3.5-plus17s938~55Too verbose ignores "one sentence" instruction
qwen3.7-max29s1427~49Batch only high tok/s but reasoning overhead

Caveat: qwen3.5 and qwen3.7 have higher tok/s but produce 30x more tokens because they reason internally before responding. Wall-clock latency is what the user experiences, so we optimized for that. A future test should measure time-to-first-token to separate queue time from generation speed. Model IDs are DashScope international identifiers (verified via API).

Why qwen-plus for fast tasks instead of qwen-max?
qwen-plus is 35% faster (1.1s vs 1.7s) and cheaper ($0.80/M vs $1.60/M input) with equivalent quality for synthesis tasks. We switched ai-read, recommend explanation, picks-narrative, and ai-strikes from qwen-max/claude-sonnet to qwen-plus. Blended cost savings (input + output) are ~73-95% depending on the route.
Why qwen3-max for reasoning instead of claude-sonnet?
qwen3-max is the newest non-thinking reasoning model at 2.4s — fast enough for real-time use. Claude Sonnet ($3/M + $15/M) costs 3x more. The 6 reasoning calls run in parallel via Promise.all(), so wall-clock time equals the slowest single call (~3s), not the sum.

5. The Reasoning Engine

The reasoning engine (api/src/agents/reasoner.ts) runs 6 parallel LLM calls against qwen3-max. Each call has a specialized system prompt and receives the full market context + actual trade legs.

6 Reasoning Tasks
#TaskQuestion AnsweredOutput Type
1ThesisWhy will this trade profit?Conviction 1-10, primary driver, structural edge, catalyst, bear case
2RiskWhat could go wrong?3-5 scenarios with probabilities, risk grade, kill switch
3ScenariosP&L at different spots/times?Grid + best/base/worst cases (probabilities only — P&L computed)
4ExitWhen to close?Profit target %, stop loss, time exit, adjustment triggers
5RegimeDoes macro support this?Market regime, sector momentum, VIX, correlation, alignment 0-1
6SizingHow much to risk?Allocation %, contracts by account size (overridden by computed max loss)

How conviction and grade are computed

totalConviction = thesis.conviction * 0.7 + regime.alignmentScore * 10 * 0.3

riskPenalty = risk.overallRiskGrade === 'EXTREME' ? 2
            : risk.overallRiskGrade === 'HIGH' ? 1 : 0

adjusted = totalConviction - riskPenalty

Grade: A (≥8), A- (≥7), B+ (≥6), B (≥5), C (≥3), D (< 3)

shouldPublish = totalConviction > 6  // i.e. B+ or above

Note on regime alignment: Regime flows into conviction at 30% weight. It is NOT applied as a separate gate on shouldPublish — that would penalize macro misalignment twice (once in conviction, once in the gate). A single conviction threshold of 6 (B+ or above) captures regime through the weighted formula.

Note on conviction calibration: thesis.conviction and scenario probabilities are LLM-produced numbers that gate shouldPublish. They're uncalibrated until the outcome feedback loop exists. Outcome tracking (correlating conviction with actual P&L) is a near-term priority — see section 13.

Caching

Results are cached for 15 minutes keyed on ticker + expiry + structure_hash. The structure hash is a SHA-1 of the normalized legs (type, side, strike, mid). This ensures a cache hit only occurs when the displayed legs are byte-identical to the reasoned ones. If the advisor picks different strikes within the TTL window, reasoning re-runs with the new structure.

// Cache key includes structure hash to prevent stale narrative
const cacheKey = `${ticker}:${expiry}:${structureHash(legs)}`;

6. Computed vs LLM — The Hard Lesson

This was the most important design lesson of the entire build. Early versions asked the LLM to generate P&L numbers for the scenarios grid and heatmap. The results were:

What the LLM generated (wrong)
Spot MoveLLM P&LCorrect P&LError
-5% ($118)-$1.24+$3.76WRONG sign + magnitude
0% ($124)+$1.76+$5.51Missed ITM long put
+5% ($130)+$1.76+$1.76Correct (all OTM)

The LLM only got the trivial case right (all puts OTM). It failed to compute intrinsic value for ITM legs — a basic options math operation.

Other LLM math failures we found:

  • "Unlimited loss" for a 4-leg put spread with long protection — a defined-risk structure can never have unlimited loss
  • Invented credit/loss numbers that contradicted the actual legs shown on the same page
  • Call-side language for put-side trades — describing "short call wing" when all legs were puts
  • Position sizing based on wrong max loss — recommending 40 contracts on a $100k account because it used an LLM-estimated max loss of $88 instead of the actual $194
The rule we derived

Never ask an LLM to do arithmetic. If the answer can be computed from a formula, compute it. Use the LLM for judgment calls (is this risk high or low?), pattern recognition (what scenario is most likely?), and natural language (explain why this trade fits). Use code for everything that has a right answer.

7. Data Flow

The Discover Pro page calls 4 API endpoints in sequence, then computes the rest client-side:

GET /api/snapshot/{ticker}
→
POST /api/recommend
→
POST /api/ai-read (direction-aware)
→
POST /api/reasoning/analyze

Why recommend runs before ai-read

In the original Discover page, ai-read runs first. But ai-read didn't know the engine's direction, so it would say "bullish, buy calls" while the engine picked a bearish BWB. We reordered the calls: recommend runs first, then ai-read receives engineDirection and engineStrategy so its market read is consistent.

Recommend is the sole pricing authority

/api/recommend is the only place where legs get priced. It fetches the option chain via Alpaca, the advisor selects strikes, then the route enriches each leg with real bid, ask, mid, and iv by matching against the chain. Mid is only computed when both bid and ask exist; otherwise the leg is marked estimated: true.

// routes/ai.ts — after advisor picks legs
for (const leg of strat.legs) {
  const match = contracts.find(c => c.strike === leg.strike && c.type === leg.type);
  if (match) {
    const hasBoth = typeof match.bid === 'number' && typeof match.ask === 'number'
      && match.bid > 0 && match.ask > 0;
    leg.mid = hasBoth ? (match.bid + match.ask) / 2 : (match.mid ?? null);
    leg.bid = match.bid ?? null;
    leg.ask = match.ask ?? null;
    leg.iv = match.iv ?? null;
    leg.estimated = !hasBoth;
  }
}

Reasoning requires enriched legs

/api/reasoning/analyze validates that all legs have real mids before proceeding. If any leg is missing a mid, the endpoint returns 400 LEGS_NOT_PRICED instead of silently estimating. This enforces one source of truth: recommend prices the legs, reasoning consumes them, the client computes P&L from them.

// routes/ai-reasoning.ts — validate before reasoning
const missingMid = legs.find(l => typeof l.mid !== 'number' || l.mid <= 0);
if (missingMid) {
  return reply.code(400).send({
    error: 'LEGS_NOT_PRICED',
    detail: `Leg ${missingMid.type}@${missingMid.strike} has no mid. `
      + `Call /api/recommend first; reasoning does not estimate prices.`,
  });
}

The client treats LEGS_NOT_PRICED as a signal to fall back to mock data with a visible warning — never as something to render around.

8. Consistency Rules

After testing, we identified 7 consistency rules that all reasoning output must follow. These are enforced through prompt engineering and post-processing:

Consistency rules enforced in prompts
  1. Direction match: If engine picks bearish BWB, thesis must explain bearish — never write a bullish thesis
  2. Actual legs: Reference the real leg types (put vs call). Never describe put legs as call legs
  3. IV data honesty: When IV Rank is unavailable, say so. Never claim "elevated IV" when IV Rank = 0
  4. Defined risk: A 4-leg spread with long protection = defined risk. Never say "unlimited loss"
  5. Single P&L source: All sections must use the same credit, max loss, and max profit numbers
  6. Exit references legs: Stop loss and adjustments must reference actual strikes and leg types
  7. BWB asymmetry: Broken wing butterfly must have unequal wing widths — validated post-parse

The prompts include a CRITICAL RULES section that explicitly states these constraints. The buildLegsContext() method adds:

CRITICAL RULES:
- This is a put-side broken wing butterfly | 4 legs | DEFINED RISK
- If all legs have long protection, loss is DEFINED — never say "unlimited loss"
- Reference the ACTUAL leg types — do not describe put legs as call legs
- Use the EXACT P&L numbers above — do not invent different credit/loss numbers

Additionally, a computed reference bar is displayed above the reasoning cards showing the real numbers. This lets the user instantly cross-reference whether the LLM text matches the math.

9. The Pricing Problem

The most persistent issue was leg pricing. Initially, /api/recommend returned legs as { type, side, strike } — no mid prices. The advisor LLM picks strikes from the chain summary, but the chain mids weren't carried through to the response. This is now fixed: recommend enriches every leg with real bid/ask/mid/iv from the chain (see §7).

What we tried

ApproachResult
Estimate mids from distance-to-spot formulaFailed Created arbitrage payoffs (no loss zone)
Scale estimated mids to match API netCreditPartial Ratios correct but absolute values wrong
Enrich legs with real chain data in recommend routeFixed Legs now include real bid/ask/mid/iv

The arbitrage detection

When estimated mids produce a structure with no loss zone (all P&L positive at every spot price), the system now flags it:

  • Sets midsAreEstimated = true
  • Shows a yellow warning banner: "Pricing notice: Mid prices are estimated. Structure shows no loss zone — this indicates stale or estimated pricing, not a real arbitrage opportunity."
  • Labels all computed values with "(estimated)" annotations

10. Post-Build Review & Fixes

After the initial build, a structured code review identified 11 issues across 3 severity tiers. All were resolved before the production test.

#IssueRoot CauseFix
1 Cache key ignores structure Cached reasoning keyed on ticker:expiry. If recommend returns different legs within 15 min (spot moved, chain shifted), the page renders fresh legs with cached narrative referencing old strikes. Cache key now includes structureHash(legs) — SHA-1 of normalized (type, side, strike, mid). Different legs = different cache entry.

Review takeaway

The most dangerous bug (#1) wasn't in the prompt or the model — it was in the cache. A 15-minute TTL keyed on ticker:expiry could serve stale reasoning that references legs the user never sees. The fix was structural: include a hash of the actual legs in the cache key, so a hit only occurs when the displayed structure matches the reasoned one. Caches that outlive the data they summarize are a class of bug, not an edge case.

12. What Worked

  • Parallel reasoning calls: 6 calls in ~3s wall-clock. This was the key performance insight — Promise.all() makes depth free in latency terms.
  • Computed P&L overriding LLM: Once we moved all math to legMath.js, the chart, heatmap, metrics strip, and position sizing table all agreed. One source of truth.
  • Computed reference bar: Showing the real numbers above the reasoning cards lets users instantly spot when LLM text contradicts math. Trust through transparency.
  • Model tiering: Using qwen-plus for narration and qwen3-max for reasoning gave us fast + deep in the same page load.
  • BWB post-parse validation: Automatically widening symmetric wings when the LLM picks equal widths. The prompt + validation belt-and-suspenders approach works better than prompt-only.
  • Direction-aware ai-read: Reordering the API calls (recommend first, then ai-read) eliminated the "bullish market read + bearish strategy" contradiction.

13. What Didn't Work

  • LLM-generated P&L numbers: The LLM cannot reliably compute multi-leg options payoffs. It gets simple cases right (all OTM) but fails on ITM legs, butterfly structures, and any trade with asymmetric wings. This cost us 2+ iterations.
  • Mid price estimation: The formula mid = spot * 0.02 * exp(-dist * 10) produces unrealistic prices. A BWB with estimated mids showed a risk-free payoff — $176-$676 profit everywhere with zero loss. This was caught by the user's mathematical audit.
  • Stale reasoning text: When the reasoning API is called with incomplete data (no legs, no mids), the LLM invents details. It would reference "$123 short call spread" when the actual trade had no calls. The fix was providing complete, labeled data with explicit "do not invent" instructions, but some hallucination persists.
  • Position sizing from LLM: The LLM recommended 40 contracts on $100k with computed max loss of $3,520 — but the real max loss was $7,760. We replaced this entirely with computed sizing based on 2% account risk.
  • Canvas payoff chart: The initial Canvas-based chart had DPI/retina scaling issues. Switching to Highcharts fixed it immediately and gave us tooltips, breakeven labels, and spot line for free.

14. What's Next

Immediate (this week)

  • Verify real mids from production: Test with live market data to confirm the recommend route now returns actual bid/ask/mid from the chain. This should eliminate all pricing warning banners.
  • Validate breakevens: With real mids, breakevens should compute correctly. Verify they match the chart's zero-crossings.
  • Test reasoning text consistency: With real legs + real mids + real P&L passed to the prompts, verify the thesis/risk/exit text references the correct strikes, direction, and numbers.
  • Outcome tracking (promoted from mid-term): Store model ID, prompt version, thesis conviction, and scenario probabilities alongside recommendation_log. These LLM-produced numbers gate shouldPublish and published Top Picks — they're as un-auditable as the P&L numbers we pulled out of the LLM, just without a chart to contradict them. The publish gate runs on uncalibrated conviction until this loop exists. Record provenance so we can attribute drift when prompts are tuned later.

Short-term (next 2 weeks)

  • Black-Scholes interim P&L: The ~7d and ~14d columns in the heatmap currently use linear interpolation (30%/65% of expiry P&L). Replace with a BS model that accounts for theta decay, vega, and gamma properly.
  • Scenario detection: Classify each ticker into one of 15 market scenarios (high IV, pre-earnings, breakout, etc.) and select only the relevant reasoning prompts. This reduces cost and improves prompt relevance.
  • Conviction calibration: With outcome data flowing, correlate conviction scores with actual P&L. Tune prompt instructions based on systematic biases (e.g., if conviction 7 trades win at the same rate as conviction 5, the scale is miscalibrated).

Mid-term (next month)

  • Conviction gate: Auto-suppress picks below conviction threshold from "Top Picks" in the Discover page. Only publish trades where both engine score AND reasoning conviction pass.
  • Portfolio-level reasoning: Before publishing a new pick, check existing positions for correlation, sector concentration, and total portfolio Greeks. The regime context already assesses macro — extend it to portfolio.
Design philosophy

Discover Pro is not a chatbot. It's a structured, opinionated analysis that takes a position: here's the trade, here's why, here's the risk, here's how to manage it, here's how much to risk. Every section either answers a specific question or provides a specific number. If a section can't be computed accurately, we label it as estimated rather than presenting it as fact.
