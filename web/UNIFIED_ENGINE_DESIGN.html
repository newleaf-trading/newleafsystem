NewLeaf System · Architecture Design Doc

One Brain, Two Eyes — Unifying the Strategy Engines

NewLeaf runs two independent deterministic engines that each classify a regime and suggest a strategy on different evidence — gamma positioning vs. price support/resistance. This doc proposes collapsing them into one decision authority with two evidence sources.

Why this doc

Two decision authorities, one user

Both engines are deterministic and both are good. The problem isn't duplication of code — it's duplication of authority. The same ticker can get two regimes and two suggested strategies on two pages, with no reconciliation.

Engine A — Gamma / Decision

  • strategy-engine.js + decision-engine.ts
  • Reads gamma walls (OI/GEX) + technicals
  • Powers /api/recommend → Discover, the scanner
  • Output: decision tier + score + risk plan + built legs

Engine B — Reaction / S-R

  • shared/reaction/ (regime, score, bias, zones…)
  • Reads support/resistance zones + touch history
  • Powers movement-range.html
  • Output: regime + setup quality + strategy bias
Coupling today = zero. strategy-engine.js imports nothing from shared/reaction/; decision-engine.ts imports nothing from it; price-reaction.ts (the typed wrapper) feeds only movement-range. They are fully parallel implementations that happen to answer overlapping questions.

04Where they disagree — on the same ticker

The overlap that matters: both classify a regime and both pick a directional spread, but on different evidence, so they can contradict.

Example — testing support vs. "neutral"

Spot just above SMA50, ADX 18, a 3-touch support zone 1% below with an 83% hold rate.

Engine A ADX<20, no strong trend → Range-bound or No-trade. Blind to the zone.

Engine B spot within 0.5×ATR of a strong zone → testing_support → bull_put_spread. Catches the bounce A misses.

Example — falling knife

ADX 28 downtrend, spot 1 ATR above a support zone.

Engine A ADX≥25 + below SMAs → Bearish trend continuation; could still suggest premium selling near the wall.

Engine B trendIntoZone=true → vetoes the trade (price is knifing into the level). A can't see this.

Example — vol read

ATM IV 22%, RV 25% (IV/RV 0.88).

Engine A IV 22% ∈ [20,50] → "fine", supports selling premium.

Engine B IV/RV < 0.95 → vol is cheap → prefers a debit/directional structure. B reads relative value; A reads absolute.

05What each sees that the other can't

Neither is redundant. Consolidation must preserve both sets of unique signals — that's the whole point of "two eyes."

Only Engine A has

  • Gamma walls from OI/GEX (B accepts walls as input, never computes them)
  • BWB strike construction (deterministic body/wings/zero-risk buffer)
  • Blended OI/GEX confidence
  • SMA-stack trend validation

Only Engine B has

  • Zone touch memory (hold/reject rates, Wilson-smoothed)
  • Position-in-range → asymmetric / skewed structures
  • Falling-knife veto (trendIntoZone)
  • Event calendar exclusion (earnings / ex-div in DTE)
  • Containment / range-quality measurement

06Target architecture — one authority, two eyes

Make decision-engine the single decision authority. Demote the reaction engine from a parallel verdict to a feature & gate provider whose zone/event signals feed the one decision.

ONE regime · score · decision · legs · risk plan
Principle: unify the decision, keep both evidence sources. The reaction engine keeps its zone math (zones, stats, premium, events); its decision role (regime.cjs verdict + bias.cjs selection) is absorbed into decision-engine as inputs and gates, not a competing answer.

Both surfaces read the same authority

Discover and movement-range stop computing their own verdicts. movement-range becomes a view over the unified decision, filtered/sorted by S-R relevance — same regime vocabulary, same suggested strategy, same score everywhere.

08Migration plan

  1. Surface reaction as features. Wire price-reaction.ts into /api/recommend: run zone/touch/containment/event analysis on the same bars + walls, expose a structured reactionFeatures object. No decision change yet — just attach the data.
  2. Add the veto + event gates. Feed trendIntoZone and earnings-in-DTE into buildDecision as downgrade/veto guardrails. Lowest-risk, highest-safety win.
  3. Add zone gates to directional selection. Require a tested rail (score ≥65) for bull_put / bear_call in strategy-engine's directional gate; use posInRange to route skewed structures.
  4. Merge the regime vocabularies into one ladder in classifyRegime (zone-relative + trend labels under one precedence).
  5. Reconcile scoring. Blend reaction's zone/event components into the engine score (or keep both and expose both), tuned later against outcome data.
  6. Repoint movement-range at the unified decision (or at minimum, the same regime/strategy labels) so the two surfaces never disagree.
  7. Retire regime.cjs's verdict role and bias.cjs as a standalone authority — their logic now lives as inputs/gates.

Each step ships independently with tests. Steps 1–2 are safe and immediately valuable (the falling-knife veto + event gate close real risk holes in Engine A).

09Risks & open decisions

  • Two score models. Engine A's 3-pillar score and B's 6-component setupQuality measure different things. Decision: blend, or keep A's tiers authoritative and use B's components as gates? (Recommend the latter first — A's tiers are production-proven — then calibrate a blend against outcomes.)
  • Regime taxonomy. Merging 8 + 7 labels risks an unwieldy ladder. Keep it to ~8 unified labels; map zone-relative states onto them rather than adding new ones for every combination.
  • Data availability. Reaction needs ≥3 touches and enough bars to form zones; on thin names it returns "unclear/untested." The unified engine must degrade gracefully (fall back to A's gamma-only path when zones are absent).
  • Performance. Running zone analysis on every /api/recommend adds compute. It's pure-function and fast, but worth measuring; the scanner already amortizes it per symbol.
  • Outcome validation. Neither score is outcome-calibrated yet. The unified score should flow into the same recommendation_snapshots stream so the blend can be tuned against real P&L.
