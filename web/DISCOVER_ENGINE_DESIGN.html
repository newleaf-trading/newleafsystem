0What Discover is

Discover takes a ticker, builds a real-time snapshot (price, RSI/MACD/ADX, gamma walls, IV term structure), scores the setup, and recommends a specific options structure — or honestly stands aside — all before the user places a trade. The recommendation is produced by a deterministic engine; an LLM is used only to narrate, never to decide.

Real-time dataGamma-wall analysis6-strategy selection Deterministic legsPass/fail decision8-agent verify (optional)

The same decision code runs on three surfaces — the Discover page (/api/recommend), the batch Scanner (report pipeline), and Movement-&-Range — so they can never disagree about the same name.

1Design principles

Deterministic

Gates, thresholds and legs are pure functions of the data. Reproducible, testable, and identical across surfaces. The LLM narrates the "why", it does not pick the trade.

Honest NO_TRADE

Standing aside is a first-class outcome that explains itself and suggests an alternative. Better an honest "wait" than a recommended trade with a mislabelled risk profile.

Defined risk

Every structure the engine ships has a capped, known loss. Exceptions to conservative rules are only ever allowed to produce defined-risk trades.

Not yet outcome-validated. The blend weights and thresholds are intuition-based. The engine discriminates — it produces a genuine regime-matched strategy distribution — but no accuracy or win-rate claim is earned until it is measured against real pick_outcomes P&L. Copy and UI must say "AI-recommended, regime-matched", never "proven" or "X% win rate".

2The pipeline, end to end

api/src/routes/ai.ts → /api/recommend

1

Snapshot & technicals

Fetch price, 400 daily bars, options chain, Nasdaq/Yahoo OI. Compute RSI/MACD/ADX/Bollinger/SMAs and the gamma walls (put wall, call wall, GEX by strike, blended confidence).

2

Engine — select & score (gate cascade)

First-pass-wins gate cascade picks one of six structures and scores it 0–100. Direction is derived from SMA alignment, so a crashed name reads neutral.

3

Reaction gate — overlay

Runs an independent support/resistance + regime analysis. It can veto (falling knife), promote a neutral pick to a directional spread, apply the quality bounce exception, or keep the pick.

4

Leg builder — construct (fail-closed)

Turns the chosen strategy into concrete strikes from the live chain. Legs must pass topology validation or nothing ships (a mislabelled structure is worse than NO_TRADE).

5

Decision engine — go / no-go

Prices the structure, classifies the regime, and returns APPROVED / WATCHLIST / NO_TRADE with a plain-English rationale and a risk plan.

3Step 1 — What we measure

Gamma walls

Where dealers hold their largest hedging positions. Put wall (floor) and call wall (ceiling) bound the expected range; the gamma flip separates the positive-gamma (mean-reverting) zone above from the negative-gamma (trend-amplifying) zone below.

Technicals

RSI (oversold/overbought), ADX (trend strength), MACD (momentum), Bollinger width (volatility regime), SMA alignment (direction). All computed from 250+ real closes — not LLM-generated.

IV & skew

ATM IV, IV/RV ratio (are options cheap or rich vs realized movement?), term structure (backwardation flags near-term event risk), expected move.

Liquidity

Open interest and volume per strike — how meaningful the walls are and whether the strikes actually trade.

The neutral trap. Direction comes only from SMA alignment (trendScore). A crashed mega-cap has "mixed" SMAs → trendScore = 0.50 → neutral → the directional gate (3) can never fire. So it lands on a BWB, which then prices as a debit and is rejected. That trap is exactly what the reaction gate's exception addresses for quality names.

Premium-adequacy penalty. A credit structure is down-scored when IV/RV < 1 (you're paid too little for the risk). Same penalty on both surfaces, so Discover and Scanner agree on the number. strategy-engine.cjs → premiumRiskPenalty()

5How "confidence" is built (and the two numbers)

Gate-driving confidence blends four signals of whether the walls are meaningful.

blendedConfidence = 0.40 × OI-absolute   // enough OI for walls to matter
                  + 0.35 × GEX-relative  // gamma concentrated at the walls
                  + 0.15 × delta         // positions actively building  (DARK today)
                  + 0.10 × volume        // strikes actually trading

Two different "confidence" numbers — don't confuse them. The UI's 86% "walls strongly defended" is this blended figure. The engine's gate and the reaction gate's wall-intact check use analysis.confidence_score — a separate gamma-reliability metric (the one the condor needs ≥0.60). For ORCL that is 0.551, not 0.86. Thresholds in this doc refer to confidence_score.

Delta is DARK. DELTA_DARK = true: the delta term is excluded from the gate-driving blend because Discover can't read OI history yet, and letting it in would make the same ticker score differently on different surfaces. Delta is still computed for diagnostics. Re-enabling it is a next-step (§11).

6Step 3 — The reaction gate

shared/reaction/gate.cjs · regime.cjs · bias.cjs — an independent second opinion built from price action around support/resistance zones.

6.1 — Regime classification (precedence order)

  1. Breakout / breakdown — spot beyond a scoreable zone.
  2. Trending — ADX > 25. Runs before proximity. If price is trending into a nearby zone, the trendIntoZone flag is set (down-into-support = falling knife; up-into-resistance = melt-up).
  3. Testing support / resistance — spot within 0.5×ATR of a tested rail.
  4. Range-bound — high containment + low ADX.
  5. Unclear — confidence capped at 40.

6.2 — The three outcomes

Veto

falling_knife
Trend into support + bias no_trade → do not sell premium into a falling knife. (Up-into-resistance → melt_up_into_resistance.)

Promote

directional
A neutral gamma pick + a directional bias at a tested rail → promote to the aligned spread (bull_put / bear_call / bull_call / bear_put).

Keep

no change
No veto, no directional bias, or an already-directional pick → keep the engine's gamma pick untouched.

Distance matters: testing a rail (within 0.5×ATR) is APPROVED-eligible; merely approaching it caps the result to WATCHLIST.

7The falling-knife exception — for a few quality stocks

Not every oversold downtrend is a knife. A durable mega-cap sitting above a defended wall mean-reverts — its selloff is usually sentiment, not a broken business. For those names only, we reinterpret the setup as a bounce.

7.1 — Who is eligible

Two groups earn the "oversold dip is a bounce" assumption — 25 names total:

  • 21 mega-cap stocks — marketCapTier === 'mega': durable, wide-moat single names (the Magnificent-7 + other blue chips).
  • 4 blue-chip ETFs — SPY, QQQ (equity indices) and GLD, SLV (major commodities): baskets that structurally mean-revert.

A small speculative single name's oversold dip is a genuine knife and gets no exception.

Eligibility gates who can qualify; the guards below decide whether it fires. Being on the list alone does nothing — the oversold + defended-wall guards still apply to every name.

ETF nuance. SPY/QQQ are the most reliable mean-reverters there are (broad equity indices with upward drift). GLD/SLV mean-revert too, but commodities can stay depressed for extended trends — so the structural-break guard (spot > put wall) and the defined-risk debit vertical matter most for those two.

7.2 — The guards (all must hold)

// shared/reaction/gate.cjs → computeReactionGate()
oversold         = rsi < 30
wallDefended     = putWall != null && spot > putWall && confidence_score >= 0.45
standAsideOrKnife = (trendIntoZone && side==='support') || bias==='no_trade' || regime==='testing_support'

qualityBounce = isQualityName && oversold && wallDefended && standAsideOrKnife
GuardProtects against
oversold (RSI<30)Firing when there is no extreme to revert from.
spot > put wallThe structural-break line. Below the defended wall = the real structure change → exception voids, even for a mega-cap.
confidence_score ≥ 0.45A noisy/thin wall reading. 0.45 is moderate — below the condor's 0.60 (a bounce needs no condor-grade wall) but above the 0.30 "weak" cutoff.
stand-aside / knife setupManufacturing a trade where the engine already has a view. We only override where there is something to override.

7.3 — What it does

When qualityBounce is true and the engine's pick is neutral, applyReactionGate — before the knife veto — promotes it to a bull call spread: a debit vertical, buy near ATM, sell toward the call wall. Flagged quality_mean_reversion. It never flips an already-directional pick.

Why a debit vertical? It matches the cheap IV/RV (buys vol rather than selling under-priced premium), its max loss is the debit ("lose a little, live to re-buy"), and being a debit it sidesteps the prices_as_debit rejection that kills the credit BWB. One structure resolves all three constraints. See the companion doc QUALITY_MEAN_REVERSION_DESIGN.html.

The list lives in two hand-synced places — the Scanner reads one, the Discover API reads the other. Both must be changed together, or the two surfaces will disagree on who is eligible.

To ADD a stock (e.g. QCOM)

// 1 + 2 — in BOTH company-metadata.json files, on the QCOM entry:
"QCOM": { "name": "Qualcomm", "sector": "Technology",
          "marketCapTier": "mega", "qualityScore": 80 }

// 3 — in api/src/tools/quality-names.ts, add to the MEGA_CAPS set:
const MEGA_CAPS = new Set([ ... , 'QCOM' ]);

To REMOVE a stock

Reverse the above: delete marketCapTier/qualityScore from both metadata files (or set the tier to large), and delete the ticker from MEGA_CAPS. It immediately stops qualifying — the guards still gate everyone else.

Then deploy — the edits are inert until you do. Discover: cd api && npm run deploy (Firebase functions). Scanner: restart the daemon launchctl kickstart -k gui/$(id -u)/com.newleaf.pipeline. Verify by re-running the ticker in Discover. Why two lists? The Cloud Function ships without the metadata JSON, so the API keeps its own mirror; collapsing these into a single build-time source is next-step §11.5.

8Steps 4–5 — Legs & the final decision

Fail-closed leg construction

leg-builder.ts snaps the chosen strategy to real strikes (nearest liquid, hard 15% distance cap, correct side). The result must pass validateStrategy topology — a symmetric fly returned as a BWB, or a debit vertical with crossed strikes, ships nothing rather than a mislabelled risk profile.

Go / no-go

decision-engine.ts → buildDecision() prices the structure from chain mids and returns one of:

OutcomeWhen
APPROVEDScore + gates pass, legs constructible, structure prices correctly, at a tested rail.
WATCHLISTSound but approaching (not testing) the rail, or a credit with thin premium (IV/RV < 1.0).
NO_TRADEFalling-knife veto · legs not constructible · a credit structure that prices as a debit (prices_as_debit) · premium too thin (IV/RV < 0.85).

Credit-only rejections apply only to CREDIT_DEFINED_RISK structures (condor, iron-fly, BWB, bull-put, bear-call). A debit vertical — like the quality-bounce bull call spread — is exempt by design.

9What the bugfixes changed

Wiring the exception surfaced three defects. All three are fixed and deployed — this is the current, correct state.

The build bug was latent and wide. It silently broke every promotion to a bull-call / bear-put debit vertical across all surfaces — not just the new exception. The quality bounce was simply the first thing to routinely emit one, which exposed a freeze that had been there since the first deploy.

10Worked example — ORCL

Spot $140.85 · RSI 12.4 · ADX 34.3 · IV/RV 0.86 · put wall $125 (5,686 put OI) · call wall $152.5 · confidence 0.551.

Before the fixes

NO_TRADE · prices_as_debit

  • Direction neutral → BWB.
  • BWB (a credit) prices as a net debit on this chain.
  • Decision engine rejects it. Stand aside.

Now

WATCHLIST · Bull Call Spread

  • qualityBounce = true — mega ✓ · RSI 12.4 ✓ · $140.85 > $125 & 0.551 ≥ 0.45 ✓ · bias no_trade ✓.
  • Promoted → bull call spread long 141 / short 152.5.
  • Debit vertical → clears the debit rejection. Approaching support → WATCHLIST.
