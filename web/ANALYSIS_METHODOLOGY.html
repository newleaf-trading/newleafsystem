1Gamma Walls & net-gamma regime

Gamma measures how fast an option's delta changes per $1 move. Market-maker (dealer) hedging of that gamma is what creates support/resistance "walls" — and its sign determines whether the market chops or trends.

Net-gamma regime NEW — the "Positive gamma · mean-reverting" banner

This is the headline addition. It answers the question from the gamma primer: is net gamma exposure positive or negative, and what does that imply? We derive it from spot vs the gamma flip:

regime = spot > gamma_flip ? POSITIVE (mean-reverting) : NEGATIVE (trending)
Net gamma regime
Positive gamma · mean-reverting
Spot is above the gamma flip. Dealers are net long gamma → they buy dips / sell rips to stay hedged → volatility is dampened and price pins between the walls. Favors range structures (condors, spreads); fade breakouts.
Net gamma regime
Negative gamma · trending
Spot is below the gamma flip. Dealers are net short gamma → they sell dips / buy rips → volatility is amplified, expect stronger trends and momentum. Favors directional / momentum setups; keep stops tight.
Net gamma regime
Balanced · at the gamma flip
Spot sits right on the flip — the regime can tip either way from here.

The banner also prints a key-levels strip: Put wall $140 (−0.7%) · Gamma flip $148 (+5.0%) · Call wall $150 (+6.4%) — each level with its signed distance from spot.

Why "positive = mean-reverting"? When dealers are long gamma, hedging opposes the move (they sell as price rises, buy as it falls), which suppresses volatility — price gravitates back toward pinned levels. When they're short gamma, hedging amplifies the move (buy higher, sell lower), which fuels trends and breakouts. This mirrors the classic gamma-exposure framework used by desks.

Volatility regime NEW — the IV/RV correctness fix

"IV level: high" alone is misleading — a stock with high IV can still be cheap if it moves even more. The banner now judges premium by IV/RV, and reads the term-structure shape:

Volatility regime
IV cheap · backwardated
ATM IV 63% vs realized 72% → IV/RV 0.87: options are cheap relative to actual movement — selling premium is under-compensated; favors buyers / debit structures. Term structure is backwardated — the front expiry sits above later weeks, signaling near-term event/fear risk.
Volatility regime
IV rich · contango
ATM IV vs realized → IV/RV > 1.15: options are expensive relative to how much the stock moves — favors net sellers (condors, spreads, BWB).

Computing IV without a greeks subscription NEW

The intraday Alpaca feed (indicative) returns no greeks/IV. Rather than depend on a paid feed, we solve implied volatility ourselves from each option's mid price using Black–Scholes (Newton–Raphson with a bisection fallback):

σ solved from: mid = BS_price(S, K, T, σ, type) // via Newton–Raphson on vega

ATM IV, the term structure, IV/RV and expected move all flow from this. If a contract has no two-sided quote, the last known-good IV is carried forward. Results refresh every 15 minutes and persist automatically.

5What changed in this release

  • Net-gamma regime banner on Gamma Walls — Positive (mean-reverting) / Negative (trending) from spot vs the gamma flip, plus a walls/flip key-levels strip.
  • Trend & momentum banner on Technical — ADX + MACD/SMA + RSI synthesis, with squeeze and earnings flags. New Bollinger %B and Support/Resistance tiles.

Honesty note. These reads describe market structure and options pricing deterministically; they are decision support, not proven edge. The recommendation engine discriminates across strategies but is not yet outcome-validated — no win-rate or accuracy claims are made.
